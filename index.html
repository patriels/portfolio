About Me

Research meets craft,
curiosity drives both.

I'm a PhD student in Computer Science at the University of Florida. My research centers around technology designed and built for people, not the other way around. My work spans a variety of backgrounds including software engineering, human-computer interaction, applied artificial intelligence and simulation.

I strive to always center the user. This ensures that the work I do isn't just motivated by innovation, but that it serves to answer the how and why of building effective technologies. I bring this user-centered focus to all my projects. Whether I'm building simulations or designing user interfaces, I ensure that my work always serves the human good.

My current work focuses on improving fall detection for older adults aging in place, using realistic simulations of elderly falls to train and evaluate AI fall detection models. I'm actively seeking collaborative opportunities where I can contribute to meaningful, real-world impact.

Simulated Data for Elderly Fall Detection

SimulationComputer VisionMachine LearningHCI

My dissertation explores whether a realistic simulation of an older adult at home can produce the training data fall detection models need to recognize how elderly people actually fall. The goal is more reliable fall detection that helps older adults age in place safely.

Research Methods

  • Planned 3 × 2 design comparing 3 models across 2 test datasets (simulated vs. the UR Fall Dataset), analyzed with logistic regression
  • Evaluation on accuracy, precision, recall, and F1, plus true/false positive and negative counts

Software Development

  • Realistic simulation of an older adult performing 4 fall-related motions in a home environment
  • 3 YOLO26-based fall detection models: a COCO-based joint-tracking model, one trained on the UR Fall Dataset, and one trained on simulated data

Llama 3.1VLMRoboticsSandia NL

Pause Posts: Mindfulness in Social Media Feeds

ExperimentSurveysFigma

Scrolling through negative news can take a toll on how people feel. Pause Posts tests whether short mindfulness prompts placed directly in a social media feed can improve users' mood while they scroll.

Research Methods

  • IRB-approved, randomized between-subjects experiment (n = 14), recruited through SONA
  • Pre/post Qualtrics surveys built on the PANAS-X affect scale, refined through 3 pilot sessions
  • Wilcoxon signed-rank and Mann-Whitney U tests in Python; positive affect rose significantly in the treatment group (p = .01)
  • Observed session time and prompt completion alongside self-reports
  • Thematic coding of open-ended responses with a second rater (Cohen’s κ = 0.81)

UI/X Artifacts

  • Figma prototype of an Instagram-style feed
  • Mindfulness prompt designs placed every 8th post

Software Development

  • Instagram-style web app with a controlled feed used to run the study

Camera Placement and Test-Taker Immersion

ExperimentLikert MeasuresQualitative Coding

Side-facing webcams can make remote proctoring more trustworthy, but students reported that camera placement affected their performance. This study tested whether a side-facing camera changes how immersed test-takers feel during a timed task.

Research Methods

  • Randomized between-subjects experiment (n = 9) comparing front- and side-facing cameras
  • Likert measures grounded in a published immersion framework
  • Mann-Whitney U tests in Python; no significant difference in immersion
  • Thematic coding of open-ended responses with a second rater (Cohen’s κ = 0.87)
  • Follows my earlier side-facing camera research (SIGCSE 2021, ASEE 2022, honors thesis)
